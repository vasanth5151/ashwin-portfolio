How the work runs.

The same five stages every time, whether I'm running a single SEO project or full-funnel marketing.

Step 01

Research

Business, competitors, search demand and current performance. No plan before the picture is clear.

Step 02

Strategy

Choose the channels that fit the funnel and the budget, and define what success will be measured against.

Step 03

Execute

Technical fixes, content, campaigns and creative, shipped in sequence, not all at once.

Step 04

Optimise

Read the data weekly. Cut what isn't converting, expand what is, and keep testing the next variable.

Step 05

Scale

Take the combinations that work and increase reach, spend or output behind them.

Those systems still retrieve from indexed pages, which means crawlability, clarity and credibility matter more than they did, not less. The traffic that does arrive tends to be further down the funnel.

You cannot optimise the model, only what it can find and verify. That means consistent facts about the brand everywhere it appears, content written in clean, quotable statements rather than padding, clear entity signals through schema and about pages, and mentions on sources these systems already trust. This is what GEO, generative engine optimisation, does in practice.

Answer engine optimisation is structuring a page so a machine can lift a complete answer straight out of it, which usually means answering the question in the first two lines and letting the detail follow.

Nine times out of ten the traffic is real but mismatched: informational keywords instead of buying ones, the wrong locations, or a page that never actually asks for the enquiry. Check conversion rate by landing page before spending anything more on traffic. Buying more of the wrong visitors is the expensive mistake.

Creative fatigue, almost always. Frequency climbs, click-through falls, cost per lead rises, and the account looks broken when the targeting is fine. The fix is a pipeline of new creative, not a bigger budget on the ad that has already been seen five times.

The direct effect is small. The indirect effect is large, because slow pages lose people before they convert, and that shows up in the numbers that matter. Fix the worst offenders, usually oversized images and render-blocking scripts, and stop chasing a perfect score.

Google's stated position targets unhelpful content, not how it was produced. In practice, unedited generic output tends to be unhelpful, says nothing new, and rarely gets cited by AI systems either. Use it as a drafting tool, then add the specifics, and evidence only a person has.

A complete, correct Google Business Profile, plus making sure the pages that sell are actually indexed and carry titles matching what people search. Those two take days rather than months and often produce the first enquiries before any content work begins.
